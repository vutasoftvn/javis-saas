**Tone**: *Direct, assertive, commercial.*

>
> "In Lesson 3.2, you will master **Willingness to Pay**. [pause 0.5s] You will learn to navigate corporate purchasing dynamics, qualify budget authority, and extract hard economic proof before writing automated billing code."

### [SLIDE 2 AUDIO] — The 4 Levels of Commercial Commitment (30s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 2 (Slide 2: Four-tier pyramid showing Compliment, Data, LOI, and Paid Deposit.)
**Tone**: *Instructional, precise.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Buying intent exists on a strict four-level ladder. [pause 0.5s] Tier 1 is polite compliments: 'This looks amazing!' That has zero commercial value."
>
> "Tier 2 is time and data commitment—when they send you real company records. [pause 0.5s] Tier 3 is a signed Letter of Intent agreeing to buy if you deliver certain features. And Tier 4 is cold, hard cash: an upfront pre-order deposit. Never declare monetization validated based on compliments alone."
>

### [SLIDE 3 AUDIO] — The Economic Buyer vs. End User (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 3 (Slide 3: User with glowing heart vs. executive with glowing gold key.)
**Tone**: *Strategic, architectural.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Understand who actually holds the power in B2B transactions. [pause 0.5s] The **End User** is the clerk or engineer who loves your UI, but they usually have zero spending authority."
>
> "The **Economic Buyer** is the VP or CFO who controls the budget. [pause 0.5s] They don't care about button colors; they care about ROI, headcount savings, and risk. You must equip your end user with the financial business case to convince their boss."
>

### [SLIDE 4 AUDIO] — WTP Tracking in COSA Sales CRM (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 4 (Slide 4: COSA Sales CRM pipeline view with Budget Verified badges.)
**Tone**: *Practical, technical.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "In COSA Sales CRM, you track willingness to pay on every deal card. [pause 0.5s] You document the name of the economic buyer, their discretionary approval limit, and their fiscal buying cycle."
>
> "COSA enforces strict qualification gates. [pause 0.5s] A deal cannot move into the proposal stage until you have confirmed that real budget exists to purchase the solution."
>

### [SLIDE 5 AUDIO] — Anti-Patterns vs. Best Practices (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 5 (Slide 5: Contrast table showing timid giveaways versus bold commercial qualification.)
**Tone**: *Cautionary, coaching.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Do not be afraid to talk about money. [pause 0.5s] Many founders hide behind free trials because they are terrified of hearing a customer say, 'That's too expensive.'"
>
> "Hearing that early is a gift! [pause 0.5s] Introduce your target price in your very first conversation. If they choke at five hundred dollars a month, you discover immediately that your value proposition needs work, while you still have runway to fix it."
>

### [SLIDE 6 AUDIO] — Founder Action Checkpoint (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 6 (Slide 6: Buyer qualification checklist card with checkmarks.)
**Tone**: *Action-oriented, closing.*
