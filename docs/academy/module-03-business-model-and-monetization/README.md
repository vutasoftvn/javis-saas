# Module 3 — Business Model and Monetization

**Purpose:** Help the founder validate willingness to pay, revenue logic, channels, and early unit economics.

**COSA surfaces:** Sales CRM, Finance, Marketing, Projects, Templates, and Tasks.

## Lesson 3.1 — Common Business-Model Patterns

**Content:** Compare subscription, transaction, marketplace, service, licensing, and hybrid models. The right model depends on value delivery, customer behavior, and operating constraints.

**COSA practice:** Record candidate business-model assumptions in Strategy.

**Learner output:** Select one primary business-model pattern and explain why.

**Learning objectives:**

- Compare common business-model patterns such as subscription, transaction, service, and marketplace models.
- Identify which model best fits a customer problem and buying behavior.
- Describe the main assumptions behind a selected business model.

## Lesson 3.2 — Revenue Hypotheses and Willingness to Pay

**Content:** Interest is not payment. Test whether a real buyer sees enough value to allocate budget, make a commitment, change a process, or accept a price.

**COSA practice:** Create buyer-interview tasks and record conversations in Sales CRM.

**Learner output:** Write a revenue hypothesis for one buyer segment.

**Learning objectives:**

- Form a revenue hypothesis for a target customer segment.
- Distinguish stated interest from actual willingness to pay.
- Design a conversation or test that reveals pricing sensitivity.

## Lesson 3.3 — Testing Pricing

**Content:** Pricing tests can use interviews, proposals, pre-orders, pilots, tier comparisons, or paid manual delivery. The goal is to learn, not to discover a perfect final price.

**COSA practice:** Track pricing experiments and customer responses in Sales CRM.

**Learner output:** Design one pricing test with a clear success signal.

**Learning objectives:**

- Select a pricing test appropriate for an early-stage venture.
- Define what a successful pricing signal looks like.
- Interpret pricing feedback without treating every objection as a pricing problem.

## Lesson 3.4 — Fundamentals of Unit Economics

**Content:** Introduce revenue, cost to serve, gross margin, acquisition cost, retention, and customer lifetime value. Early numbers are assumptions, but they should be visible and reviewed.

**COSA practice:** Build a simple financial model in Finance or a template.

**Learner output:** Create a first unit-economics estimate with stated assumptions.

**Learning objectives:**

- Explain revenue, gross margin, acquisition cost, and customer lifetime value.
- Identify the assumptions behind an early unit-economics model.
- Use unit economics to evaluate whether a model can become sustainable.

## Lesson 3.5 — Defining a Revenue Metric Contract

**Content:** A metric is useful only when its definition, data source, owner, calculation, and review cadence are clear. This prevents teams from debating numbers instead of making decisions.

**COSA practice:** Define revenue metrics and review cadence in Finance or Strategy.

**Learner output:** Document three revenue metrics with formulas and owners.

**Learning objectives:**

- Choose the revenue metrics that matter for the current business model.
- Define how each metric is calculated and reviewed.
- Avoid vanity metrics that do not support a business decision.

## Lesson 3.6 — Identifying Revenue-Model Risks

**Content:** Revenue models fail through low conversion, poor retention, high delivery cost, long sales cycles, weak channels, or dependency on one customer. Prioritize risks by impact and uncertainty.

**COSA practice:** Add business-model risks to Project review and decision records.

**Learner output:** Create a ranked list of five business-model risks.

**Learning objectives:**

- Identify operational, pricing, retention, and channel risks in a revenue model.
- Rank risks by likelihood and business impact.
- Create an experiment that reduces the highest-priority risk.

## Lesson 3.7 — Optimizing Sales Channels

**Content:** Compare direct sales, partnerships, content, community, outbound, paid acquisition, and product-led channels. The early objective is efficient learning, not scaling every channel.

**COSA practice:** Build a small pipeline in Sales CRM and track one channel experiment.

**Learner output:** Select one primary sales channel for a four-week experiment.

**Learning objectives:**

- Compare potential acquisition and sales channels for a target segment.
- Identify the channel with the best early learning potential.
- Design a simple sales experiment for one channel.

## Lesson 3.8 — Synthesizing Business-Model Evidence

**Content:** Review pricing, buyer reactions, pipeline, channel performance, costs, and risks. The output is a decision: pilot, revise the model, narrow the segment, or stop.

**COSA practice:** Create a business-model review in Strategy or Project knowledge.

**Learner output:** Produce a business-model evidence summary.

**Learning objectives:**

- Summarize evidence for demand, pricing, channel, and unit economics.
- Identify the assumptions that remain unproven.
- Decide whether the venture is ready for a controlled pilot.

## Lesson 3.9 — Preparing a Customer Pilot

**Content:** A pilot is a controlled learning engagement with real customers. It needs clear scope, success criteria, operating boundaries, and communication expectations.

**COSA practice:** Create pilot tasks, a customer record, and a review cadence.

**Learner output:** Draft a pilot charter for one customer or customer group.

**Learning objectives:**

- Define the purpose and scope of a customer pilot.
- Select pilot customers who can provide meaningful learning.
- Prepare the operational conditions needed to run a pilot safely.
