# Module 4 — Pilot and Go-to-Market Execution

**Purpose:** Help founders operate a real pilot, learn from data, and decide whether to expand, revise, pause, or stop.

**COSA surfaces:** Tasks, Workflows, Approvals, Agents, Sales CRM, Marketing, and Projects.

## Lesson 4.1 — Designing a Controlled Pilot

**Content:** A good pilot is narrow enough to learn quickly and real enough to expose operational reality. It needs a target customer, scope, timeframe, owner, success criteria, and boundaries.

**COSA practice:** Create a pilot plan with tasks and milestones.

**Learner output:** Produce a pilot one-pager.

**Learning objectives:**

- Define a pilot with a clear customer group, duration, and learning goal.
- Separate pilot success criteria from long-term growth metrics.
- Create a pilot plan that minimizes operational complexity.

## Lesson 4.2 — Defining Pilot Metrics

**Content:** Separate activity metrics from learning metrics. Pilot metrics should reveal activation, usage, customer outcome, support burden, delivery quality, and willingness to continue.

**COSA practice:** Set up a pilot metric review in Strategy or Finance.

**Learner output:** Define five pilot metrics and their weekly review questions.

**Learning objectives:**

- Select leading and lagging metrics for a pilot.
- Define a metric contract with a source, calculation, cadence, and owner.
- Use pilot metrics to support a go or no-go decision.

## Lesson 4.3 — Managing Beta Customer Relationships

**Content:** Beta customers need clear expectations: what is experimental, what support they receive, what feedback is requested, and how changes will be communicated.

**COSA practice:** Use Sales CRM and Tasks to create a beta-customer communication cadence.

**Learner output:** Draft a beta-customer onboarding and feedback plan.

**Learning objectives:**

- Set clear expectations with beta customers.
- Establish a feedback cadence that protects customer trust.
- Turn customer requests into structured learning rather than uncontrolled scope.

## Lesson 4.4 — Analyzing Pilot Data Weekly

**Content:** Weekly analysis asks what happened, why it happened, what changed for the customer, and what action is next. The goal is a learning rhythm, not a slide deck.

**COSA practice:** Run a weekly review using Hub, Tasks, Project data, and metric views.

**Learner output:** Complete one weekly pilot review template.

**Learning objectives:**

- Review pilot data using a consistent weekly operating rhythm.
- Identify meaningful patterns, anomalies, and missing information.
- Convert data review into a prioritized action list.

## Lesson 4.5 — Making a Pilot Go or No-Go Decision

**Content:** Founders need pre-defined decision criteria so they do not reinterpret weak results. The decision can be go, continue with changes, pause, narrow the scope, or stop.

**COSA practice:** Record a decision with rationale and follow-up actions.

**Learner output:** Write a go, revise, pause, or stop recommendation.

**Learning objectives:**

- Define decision criteria before reviewing pilot results.
- Evaluate evidence without ignoring negative signals.
- Communicate a go, revise, pause, or stop decision clearly.

## Lesson 4.6 — Synthesizing Pilot Evidence

**Content:** Gather customer feedback, operational records, usage, revenue signals, and team observations into one pilot review. Highlight what should be trusted and what still needs validation.

**COSA practice:** Store a pilot review in the Project knowledge base.

**Learner output:** Produce a pilot evidence summary for stakeholders.

**Learning objectives:**

- Consolidate pilot results from customer feedback, operations, and metrics.
- Identify the strongest evidence and the largest remaining risk.
- Produce a concise pilot learning summary for stakeholders.

## Lesson 4.7 — Preparing a Go-to-Market Plan

**Content:** A go-to-market plan is an executable learning plan for reaching a defined customer, with a message, offer, channel, sales motion, and measurement approach.

**COSA practice:** Use Marketing, Sales CRM, Tasks, and Workflows to outline the first go-to-market motion.

**Learner output:** Draft an initial go-to-market plan.

**Learning objectives:**

- Define a focused target market and initial market message.
- Choose the first channels, offers, and sales motions to test.
- Build a practical go-to-market plan with measurable milestones.

## Lesson 4.8 — Assessing Product-Market-Fit Readiness

**Content:** A successful pilot does not automatically mean product-market fit. Look for repeated customer value, repeatable demand, retention signals, and an improving delivery model.

**COSA practice:** Review the Project, Sales, and Marketing evidence together.

**Learner output:** List the evidence supporting and challenging PMF readiness.

**Learning objectives:**

- Recognize the early signals that may indicate product-market fit.
- Identify why repeatable demand is different from one successful pilot.
- Define the evidence needed before increasing growth investment.

## Lesson 4.9 — Preparing for Product-Market Fit

**Content:** Prepare a stronger operating system for early growth: customer success, repeatable sales, onboarding, basic metrics, weekly reviews, and carefully selected team support.

**COSA practice:** Build a next-cycle plan in 12-Week Year and Tasks.

**Learner output:** Define the next three questions for the PMF phase.

**Learning objectives:**

- Identify the operating capabilities required for early growth.
- Define the next customer, retention, and channel questions to answer.
- Prepare a learning agenda for the product-market-fit stage.
