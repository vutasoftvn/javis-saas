# Module 5 — Product-Market Fit and Early Growth

**Purpose:** Help founders turn early customer value into repeatable acquisition, retention, sales, and operating rhythms.

**COSA surfaces:** Marketing Cockpit, Sales CRM, OKRs, 12-Week Year, Organization, Tasks, and Workflows.

## Lesson 5.1 — Defining and Measuring Product-Market Fit

**Content:** Product-market fit means a product creates enough value for a market that demand and retention can become repeatable. It is not a vanity metric, viral post, or one enthusiastic customer.

**COSA practice:** Connect PMF signals to OKRs, Sales data, and customer feedback.

**Learner output:** Define three PMF signals relevant to the venture.

**Learning objectives:**

- Define product-market fit as repeatable customer value and demand.
- Select evidence that indicates pull from the market.
- Avoid declaring product-market fit based on isolated success stories.

## Lesson 5.2 — Cohort and Retention Analysis

**Content:** Cohorts help founders see whether customers continue to receive value over time. Introduce activation, retention periods, churn, and behavioral patterns.

**COSA practice:** Review customer data and create a basic cohort or retention view.

**Learner output:** Identify one retention question and one cohort to study.

**Learning objectives:**

- Explain why cohort analysis is essential for understanding retention.
- Build a simple retention view for a product or service.
- Identify behaviors that predict long-term customer value.

## Lesson 5.3 — Building an NPS and CSAT System

**Content:** NPS and CSAT are useful only when paired with qualitative follow-up and action. Cover when to ask, what to ask, and how to close the feedback loop.

**COSA practice:** Build a feedback workflow and follow-up tasks.

**Learner output:** Draft a customer feedback survey and response plan.

**Learning objectives:**

- Distinguish NPS, CSAT, and qualitative customer feedback.
- Design a lightweight customer-feedback system.
- Turn customer feedback into prioritized product and service actions.

## Lesson 5.4 — Optimizing Acquisition Channels

**Content:** Compare growth channels by customer quality, conversion, cost, speed of learning, and retention—not top-of-funnel volume alone.

**COSA practice:** Use Marketing and Sales CRM to track one channel experiment.

**Learner output:** Create a channel scorecard for three acquisition channels.

**Learning objectives:**

- Measure channel performance using quality, conversion, and retention signals.
- Compare acquisition channels using a consistent experiment framework.
- Reallocate effort toward channels with repeatable learning.

## Lesson 5.5 — Creating a Sales Playbook

**Content:** A sales playbook documents how the team finds, qualifies, understands, proposes to, and follows up with potential customers. It enables learning to become repeatable execution.

**COSA practice:** Configure a simple sales pipeline and sales tasks.

**Learner output:** Draft a five-stage sales playbook.

**Learning objectives:**

- Define the stages of a repeatable sales process.
- Create a sales playbook for discovery, qualification, proposal, and follow-up.
- Use CRM data to improve sales execution over time.

## Lesson 5.6 — Running Structured Growth Experiments

**Content:** Growth experiments begin with a hypothesis, target behavior, success criterion, and stop rule. Focus on repeatable learning rather than uncontrolled growth hacking.

**COSA practice:** Create a growth experiment Project or task sequence.

**Learner output:** Design one growth experiment with a decision rule.

**Learning objectives:**

- Form a growth hypothesis linked to a specific customer behavior.
- Design an experiment with a measurable success criterion.
- Scale only the experiments that produce repeatable results.

## Lesson 5.7 — Managing Early Customer Success

**Content:** Customer success protects the value customers expected when they bought. Cover onboarding, adoption, outcome tracking, risk signals, and proactive support.

**COSA practice:** Create customer-success workflows, reminders, and owner tasks.

**Learner output:** Define an early customer-success journey.

**Learning objectives:**

- Define the customer outcome that customer success should protect.
- Build a simple onboarding and follow-up process.
- Identify early churn risks before they become lost customers.

## Lesson 5.8 — Building the Team and Operating Cadence

**Content:** Growth requires role clarity and consistent operating rhythms. Establish weekly priorities, reviews, decisions, accountability, and carefully scoped AI support.

**COSA practice:** Use Organization, 12-Week Year, Tasks, and Approvals to establish a team rhythm.

**Learner output:** Define a weekly operating cadence for the founder and team.

**Learning objectives:**

- Identify the roles needed for the next stage of growth.
- Establish a weekly operating cadence for priorities, decisions, and reviews.
- Use AI agents and workflows with clear human accountability.

## Lesson 5.9 — Preparing for Scale

**Content:** Before scaling, identify what is repeatable, what is fragile, what depends on individuals, and where quality or governance might break.

**COSA practice:** Review operational risks in Projects, Finance, Organization, and Workflows.

**Learner output:** Create a scale-readiness checklist.

**Learning objectives:**

- Identify which processes are repeatable enough to scale.
- Recognize the operational risks created by growth.
- Define the capabilities required before entering a scale phase.
