### [SLIDE 2 AUDIO] — The Anatomy of a Cohort Matrix (30s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 2 (Slide 2: Four cohort rows with green percentage badges: 100%, 84%, 76%, 74%.)
**Tone**: *Instructional, structured.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "A cohort matrix tracks customer groups across time. [pause 0.5s] Month 0 is one hundred percent—the accounts that signed up that month. Month 1 shows your first drop-off, which measures onboarding friction."
>
> "By Month 3, something magical should happen: the curve should stop falling and stabilize into a flat horizontal line. [pause 0.5s] That flat line is your asymptote—your permanent baseline of loyal power users. If the curve flattens above seventy percent, you have world-class product stickiness."
>

### [SLIDE 3 AUDIO] — The 3 Retention Curve Profiles (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 3 (Slide 3: Red crashing line vs. flat cyan line vs. glowing gold U-shaped smile curve.)
**Tone**: *Diagnostic, sharp.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "There are three distinct curve shapes in software. [pause 0.5s] The **Bleeding Curve** drops steadily to zero—that means the product has no lasting value and your venture is doomed unless you redesign it."
>
> "The **Flat Asymptote** drops initially and then stabilizes at sixty to seventy percent—that is healthy Product-Market Fit. [pause 0.5s] And the holy grail is the **Smiling Curve**—where existing customers upgrade seats and expand usage so much that your net revenue actually grows over time."
>

### [SLIDE 4 AUDIO] — Cohort Analytics in COSA Strategy & Finance (25s)

>
> "You can see your cohort dollar retention at a glance. [pause 0.5s] If your NRR is above one hundred and ten percent, your company is growing even before you acquire a single new customer this month."
>

### [SLIDE 5 AUDIO] — Anti-Patterns vs. Best Practices (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 5 (Slide 5: Contrast table showing blended averages versus granular weekly cohort curves.)
**Tone**: *Cautionary, mentoring.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Never trust blended churn averages. [pause 0.5s] If you add one hundred new customers every month, a massive forty percent first-month cancellation rate can easily be hidden inside a healthy-looking overall company churn number."
>
> "Look at your cohorts granularly. [pause 0.5s] Find the exact week where customer drop-off occurs. If forty percent leave in Week 2, your onboarding experience is broken. Fix the onboarding bottleneck, and watch your entire cohort curve lift."
>
