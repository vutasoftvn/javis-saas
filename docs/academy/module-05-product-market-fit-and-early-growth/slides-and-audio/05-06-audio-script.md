> **Spoken Script**:

>
> "Score it on Confidence: how certain are you based on customer data? [pause 0.5s] And score it on Ease: can you launch it in forty-eight hours? Average the three scores. Execute the highest-scoring tests every single Monday."

### [SLIDE 3 AUDIO] — The Anatomy of a Growth Experiment Card (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 3 (Slide 3: Chaotic red dice roll vs. precision glowing teal beaker measuring reaction.)
**Tone**: *Sharp, analytical.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Never launch an experiment without a falsifiable hypothesis. [pause 0.5s] Changing a button color because you feel like it is gambling; it teaches you nothing."
>
> "Frame your test scientifically: 'We believe changing our sales headline to focus on time savings will increase demo bookings from three percent to five percent over the next two hundred visitors.' [pause 0.5s] When you define the target number in advance, every test yields definitive commercial intelligence."
>

### [SLIDE 5 AUDIO] — Anti-Patterns vs. Best Practices (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 5 (Slide 5: Contrast table showing multi-variable chaos versus isolated single-variable testing.)
**Tone**: *Cautionary, mentoring.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Isolate your variables with extreme discipline. [pause 0.5s] If you change your pricing, rewrite your landing page, and launch a new email campaign all in the same week, you will have no idea what caused the result."

> "Change one variable at a time. [pause 0.5s] Test one specific headline, or test one specific email subject line. When you isolate variables, you create permanent, cumulative knowledge that compounds week over week."
