### Slide 2: The Think-Aloud Protocol (Interview Methodology)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-04 — Focus Framework`
- **Layout & Composition**: 3-stage horizontal process card stack on surface #0D172A.
- **Header Badge**: `METHODOLOGY`
- **Main Headline**: **The Think-Aloud Interview Protocol**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Turning implicit mental confusion into explicit verbal feedback.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Stage 1: Framing & Priming — 'We are testing this prototype, not you. You cannot do anything wrong. Please think out loud continuously.'
  - Stage 2: Scenario Prompt — Give them a concrete goal: 'Imagine it's Friday afternoon and you need to verify this batch of invoices. Show me how you would do that here.'
  - Stage 3: Neutral Probing — When they pause or hesitate, use the magic prompt: 'What are you thinking right now?' or 'What did you expect to happen when you clicked that?'
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: CRITICAL PROMPT: 'What did you expect to see?' reveals the mental model mismatch instantly.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Three cards showing Framing, Scenario, and Neutral Probing with speech bubble icons.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Three sleek glass cards on dark background, glowing speech bubble icons with quotes in cyan and teal typography.*

### Slide 3: The 3 Friction Signals (Diagnostic Taxonomy)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-02 — Definition Contrast`
- **Layout & Composition**: 3-column comparative container breakdown.
- **Header Badge**: `FRICTION TAXONOMY`
- **Main Headline**: **The 3 Types of Prototype Breakdown**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Diagnosing why a customer stumbles during the test.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - 1. Comprehension Breakdown: The user does not understand what a term, icon, or screen means ('What does Batch Reconciliation mean?').
  - 2. Usability Breakdown: The user understands what they want to do, but cannot locate the control or button ('Where do I upload the file?').
  - 3. Value Breakdown: The user successfully completes the task, but feels no relief ('Okay, I did that, but it didn't really save me any time over Excel').
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: CRITICAL SEVERITY: Value breakdowns are fatal. Comprehension and usability breakdowns are easily fixed with better copy.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Three vertical containers with color-coded severity tags: Yellow (Usability), Amber (Comprehension), Crimson (Value Failure).
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Three vertical cards on dark canvas #070C18, distinct hazard badges: yellow gear, amber question mark, glowing red broken heart.*

### Slide 5: Anti-Patterns vs. Best Practices (Comparative Matrix)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-06 — Decision Checkpoint`
- **Layout & Composition**: Side-by-side comparison table.
- **Header Badge**: `PROTOTYPE PITFALLS`
- **Main Headline**: **Defensiveness vs. Forensic Curiosity**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: How founders react when users struggle with their prototype.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Trap: Getting defensive ('It's obvious where the button is, you just missed it!').
  - Trap: Asking 'Did you like it?' at the end of the session. (Polite users will always say yes).

- **Highlight / Accent Box**: THE KILLER QUESTION: 'What would prevent you from using this in your real daily work tomorrow?'
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Table comparing defensive reactions with curiosity-driven investigation prompts.
