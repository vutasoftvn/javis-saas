### Slide 1: Title & Core Thesis (Hero Presentation)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-01 — Takeaway Claim`
- **Layout & Composition**: Hero presentation on #070C18 with glowing minimalist skateboard graphic.
- **Header Badge**: `COSA ACADEMY · MODULE 02 · LESSON 2.2`
- **Main Headline**: **Designing a Minimum Viable Product: The Leanest Test**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: An MVP is not a half-built product; it is the smallest testable experience that maximizes validated learning.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - The common misunderstanding: Building a buggy, stripped-down version of a car (wheels without an engine).
  - The true MVP philosophy: Building a skateboard—a complete, functional experience that solves the transportation job simply.
  - An MVP can be a concierge service, a landing page test, a paper prototype, or an interactive Figma mockup.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: MVP DEFINITION: The smallest artifact you can put in front of a customer that tests your riskiest assumption.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Classic Henrick Kniberg skateboard-to-car evolution diagram reimagined in sleek glowing neon cyan and teal on dark background.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Evolution graphic on dark canvas #070C18: Top row showing unusable wheel with red X; bottom row showing glowing teal skateboard, scooter, and car with green checkmarks.*

### Slide 2: The 4 MVP Archetypes (Taxonomy & Architecture)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-04 — Focus Framework`
- **Layout & Composition**: 4-quadrant structured grid on surface #0D172A.
- **Header Badge**: `MVP ARCHETYPES`
- **Main Headline**: **The 4 Low-Code / No-Code MVP Archetypes**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Selecting the fastest vehicle to test your solution assumption.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - 1. The Concierge MVP: Delivering the entire service manually behind the scenes without any automation (e.g., Wealthfront running portfolios manually).
  - 2. The Wizard of Oz MVP: A front-end interface that looks automated, while the founder manually performs the back-end tasks (e.g., Zappos buying shoes from local stores).
  - 3. The Interactive Click-Through: A clickable Figma or ProtoPie prototype simulating the complete workflow without a database.
  - 4. The Single-Feature Micro-App: A stripped-down codebase with exactly ONE functional button that delivers the core mechanism.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: SELECTION RULE: Choose the lowest-fidelity archetype that still allows the customer to experience the core mechanism.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Four modern UI cards in 2x2 grid with icon badges: Hand (Concierge), Wand (Wizard of Oz), Wireframe (Clickable), Laser (Single-Feature).
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *2x2 grid on deep navy canvas, glowing teal borders, minimalist icons representing concierge, wizard, wireframe, and micro-app.*

### Slide 3: Defining the Scope Perimeter (Scope Governance)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-02 — Definition Contrast`
- **Layout & Composition**: Two-panel container: Must Have vs. Explicitly Excluded.
- **Header Badge**: `SCOPE PERIMETER`
- **Main Headline**: **Enforcing the Strict MVP Scope Boundary**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: How to aggressively trim non-essential features to achieve a 14-day launch.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Must Include: The single critical workflow required to test the hypothesis and solve the core pain.
  - Explicitly Excluded: User profile customization, password resets, dark mode toggles, payment automation, multi-language support.
  - The Replacement Rule: Anything that can be handled via email, manual database updates, or phone calls MUST be excluded from code.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: DISCIPLINE: If you cannot build and test your MVP within 14 days, your scope is too large.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Split visual: Green illuminated core perimeter labeled '14-Day MVP' surrounded by an outer ring of crossed-out distraction features.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Perimeter diagram on dark slate #070C18: glowing teal circular core with 3 essential tasks, shielded from floating grey non-essential feature blocks.*

### Slide 4: MVP Project Tasks in COSA (COSA Workspace Integration)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-05 — Example Artifact`
- **Layout & Composition**: UI card preview of COSA Project Task Board with MVP Scope Boundary tag.
- **Header Badge**: `COSA IMPLEMENTATION`
- **Main Headline**: **Managing MVP Sprints in COSA Tasks**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Tagging and timeboxing MVP deliverables in your weekly execution rhythm.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - MVP Scope Tag: Every task is tagged as either #CoreMechanism or #DeferredToP2.
  - Timebox Lock: The MVP Project Bet is locked to a strict 2-week sprint window.
  - Success Criteria Card: Explicitly connects the MVP deliverable to the test plan in Lesson 2.3.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: COSA SAFEGUARD: Any task created without an explicit assumption link is flagged as feature creep.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Mockup of COSA Task Board showing filtered #CoreMechanism tasks with 14-day sprint countdown timer.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Modern Kanban board preview on dark background, glowing teal task cards, visible 'Sprint Countdown: 9 Days' indicator.*

### Slide 5: Anti-Patterns vs. Best Practices (Comparative Matrix)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-06 — Decision Checkpoint`
- **Layout & Composition**: Side-by-side comparison table.
- **Header Badge**: `MVP PITFALLS`
- **Main Headline**: **The 'Polished Product' Trap vs. True Lean MVP**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Why founders spend months building things that customers don't want.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Trap: Delaying launch because 'it's not ready yet' or 'it doesn't look professional enough.'
  - Trap: Believing that an MVP must be coded software rather than a manual service experiment.
  - Best Practice: Reid Hoffman's rule: 'If you are not embarrassed by the first version of your product, you launched too late.'
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: DECISION CHECKPOINT: If a customer won't forgive early UI flaws to solve their acute problem, the problem wasn't urgent.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Table comparing perfectionist procrastination with rapid empirical experimentation.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Comparison table on dark canvas: red hazard badges next to perfectionist delays; teal checkmarks next to rapid 14-day prototype tests.*

### Slide 6: Founder Action Checkpoint (Action Deliverable)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-07 — Learner Action`
- **Layout & Composition**: Action deliverable card container.
- **Header Badge**: `EXERCISE: MVP BOUNDARY CARD`
- **Main Headline**: **Define Your 14-Day MVP Boundary in COSA**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Select your MVP archetype and list your in-scope vs. out-of-scope features.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Step 1: Open COSA Projects and select your MVP Experiment Bet.
  - Step 2: Choose one of the 4 Archetypes (Concierge, Wizard of Oz, Clickable Prototype, or Micro-App).
  - Step 3: List exactly 3 features that are IN scope and 5 features that are EXCLUDED.
  - Step 4: Commit to delivering the testable artifact in under 14 days.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: DELIVERABLE: Publish your MVP Boundary Document in COSA Vault and schedule your testing sprint.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Interactive card preview with two-column In-Scope / Out-of-Scope lists and 14-day timer badge.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Clean digital card mockup on dark navy #070C18, showing two columns with glowing green checkmarks and red crossmarks.*
