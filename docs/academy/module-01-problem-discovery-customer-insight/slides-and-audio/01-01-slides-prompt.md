### Slide 1: Title & Core Thesis (Hero Presentation)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-01 — Takeaway Claim`
- **Layout & Composition**: Centered hero layout on #070C18 dark canvas with radial navy glow #0B1934 and bright cyan accent #38BDF8.

- **Main Headline**: **The Problem Discovery Framework**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Proving that a specific customer suffers an acute, costly problem before writing a single line of solution code.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Founders fall in love with solutions; successful businesses fall in love with painful customer problems.
  - An unvalidated problem leads to the most expensive startup failure: building a product nobody needs.
  - True problem discovery anchors on observed historical behavior, active workarounds, and quantifiable friction.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: DISCOVERY AXIOM: If customers are not already actively trying to solve the problem with clunky workarounds, the problem is not urgent.

### Slide 2: The 5 Pillars of Problem Architecture (Framework & Model)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-04 — Focus Framework`
- **Layout & Composition**: 5-card horizontal sequential container stack.
- **Header Badge**: `PROBLEM ANATOMY`
- **Main Headline**: **The 5 Essential Elements of Real Pain**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Every valid problem statement must define these five structural dimensions.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - 1. Customer Persona: Who specifically experiences the pain with highest urgency?
  - 2. Trigger Context: In what exact operational moment or situation does the pain strike?
  - 3. The Blocked Job: What desired outcome is the customer attempting to achieve?
  - 4. The Current Workaround: What manual, ugly tool (spreadsheets, paper, manual scripts) do they use today?
  - 5. The Consequence: What measurable damage occurs when the job fails? (Lost revenue, wasted hours, compliance risk).
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: DIAGNOSTIC FORMULA: Customer + Context + Job + Current Workaround + Quantified Consequence.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Five glowing rectangular nodes connected by directional arrows along a horizontal pipeline.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Five sleek glassmorphic cards in horizontal alignment on deep navy, glowing cyan connecting lines, clean iconography.*

### Slide 3: The Solution-First Trap (Problem Context & Risk)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-02 — Definition Contrast`
- **Layout & Composition**: Split comparative card layout on surface #0D172A.
- **Header Badge**: `FOUNDER BLINDSPOT`
- **Main Headline**: **Solution-First Bias vs. Customer-First Discovery**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Why starting with a product idea blinds founders to reality.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Solution-First (High Risk): 'I built an AI tool for podcast summaries; let me go find someone who wants it.'
  - Customer-First (COSA Method): 'I observed 15 content creators spending 12 hours a week manually extracting quotes and losing audience retention.'
  - The Danger: Pitching your solution prematurely biases customer feedback into false politeness.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: WARNING: Never pitch your solution in a discovery interview. You are a detective, not a salesperson.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Split visual: Red warning barrier around product mockup on left; bright teal observational lens on right.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Two column graphic: Left shows crossed-out glowing red app wireframe; right shows glowing teal forensic magnifying glass examining data points.*

### Slide 4: COSA Discovery Project Workspace (Platform Workflow)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-05 — Example Artifact`
- **Layout & Composition**: Document preview showing Problem Record card in COSA Vault.
- **Header Badge**: `COSA IMPLEMENTATION`
- **Main Headline**: **Recording Problem Statements in COSA Strategy**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Capturing evidence-backed customer pain in your permanent knowledge repository.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Strategy Canvas: Map the primary customer persona to their critical daily friction.
  - Vault Knowledge Base: Store problem records with links to raw customer interview audio and transcripts.
  - Confidence Scoring: Rate problem severity from Low (mild annoyance) to Existential (customer will pay immediately).
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: DATA REPOSITORY: Store real quotes and time-stamped evidence, never founder interpretations.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Mockup of COSA Problem Record document card with severity badge and evidence links.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Clean digital card preview on dark background #070C18, showing structured fields for Customer, Trigger, Pain, and Evidence Quote.*

### Slide 5: Anti-Patterns vs. Best Practices (Comparative Matrix)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-06 — Decision Checkpoint`
- **Layout & Composition**: Side-by-side comparison table.
- **Header Badge**: `DISCOVERY DISCIPLINE`
- **Main Headline**: **False Validation vs. Empirical Proof**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Distinguishing genuine market demand from polite noise.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Trap: Asking 'Would you use a tool that does X?' → Result: 90% say yes, 0% actually pay.
  - Trap: Talking to friends and family → Result: Biased praise that protects your feelings.
  - Best Practice: Ask 'When was the last time this broke, and what did you spend to fix it?'
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: DECISION RULE: If the customer has not spent time or money trying to fix it in the last 90 days, it is not a P0 problem.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Table comparing hypothetical validation questions with behavioral past-tense questions.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Comparison table on dark canvas: red hazard badges for hypothetical questions; teal checkmark badges for past-behavior questions.*

### Slide 6: Founder Action Checkpoint (Action Deliverable)
