# Module 6 — Scale Operations and Governance

**Purpose:** Help founders build a scalable organization with financial discipline, accountable decision-making, trusted data, risk controls, and sustainable execution.

**COSA surfaces:** Organization, Finance, Legal, Settings, Skills, Workflows, Workspace Runtime, and Approvals.

## Lesson 6.1 — Designing a Scalable Organization

**Content:** A scalable organization is built around outcomes, ownership, decision rights, and communication rhythms. Avoid premature hierarchy while making accountability visible.

**COSA practice:** Review Organization structure and clarify owners for important work.

**Learner output:** Create a simple responsibility map for the venture.

**Learning objectives:**

- Design an organization around outcomes, ownership, and decision rights.
- Distinguish between role clarity and unnecessary hierarchy.
- Identify the first organizational bottlenecks created by growth.

## Lesson 6.2 — Setting OKRs at Scale

**Content:** As teams grow, OKRs align decisions across functions. Use a small number of outcome-focused company and team objectives rather than a large reporting system.

**COSA practice:** Create company and team OKRs with review cadence.

**Learner output:** Draft one company Objective and two aligned team Objectives.

**Learning objectives:**

- Write outcome-based objectives and measurable key results.
- Connect team OKRs to strategic priorities.
- Run an OKR review without turning it into a status-reporting ritual.

## Lesson 6.3 — Managing P&L and Advanced Unit Economics

**Content:** Explain how revenue, gross margin, operating costs, cash, customer economics, and growth investment shape decisions. Make trade-offs visible before they become emergencies.

**COSA practice:** Review Finance views and define a monthly financial review.

**Learner output:** Create a P&L review checklist and three decision questions.

**Learning objectives:**

- Read a basic profit-and-loss view for a growing venture.
- Connect unit economics to pricing, retention, and operational decisions.
- Identify the financial assumptions that require regular review.

## Lesson 6.4 — Building Data and Analytics Systems

**Content:** Good analytics begins with decisions, not dashboards. Cover source ownership, metric definitions, refresh cadence, data quality, and responsible use of AI summaries.

**COSA practice:** Define metric sources and review routines in Strategy or Finance.

**Learner output:** Build a trusted-metrics register.

**Learning objectives:**

- Define the decisions that data and analytics should support.
- Establish trusted sources for key operating metrics.
- Balance speed of reporting with data quality and governance.

## Lesson 6.5 — Leading and Managing Teams

**Content:** Leadership at scale creates clarity, feedback, accountability, and conditions for good decisions. Delegate outcomes, not only isolated tasks.

**COSA practice:** Use Organization, Tasks, and Approvals to clarify ownership and decision rights.

**Learner output:** Create a delegation plan for one recurring responsibility.

**Learning objectives:**

- Create a management rhythm for feedback, decisions, and accountability.
- Identify when a founder should delegate ownership.
- Build a culture that learns from evidence and operational mistakes.

## Lesson 6.6 — Fundraising and Investor Relations

**Content:** Fundraising should support a business strategy, not replace one. Cover investor fit, narrative, metrics, diligence, update cadence, and responsible communication.

**COSA practice:** Organize investor materials, assumptions, and update tasks in Vault and Projects.

**Learner output:** Draft a one-page investor update.

**Learning objectives:**

- Explain when fundraising is appropriate for a venture.
- Prepare a clear investor narrative grounded in business evidence.
- Manage investor communication with transparency and consistency.

## Lesson 6.7 — Planning Market Expansion

**Content:** Expansion changes more than geography. It affects customers, channels, pricing, compliance, operations, support, and product expectations. Experiment before making a large commitment.

**COSA practice:** Create a market-expansion Project with assumptions and risks.

**Learner output:** Produce a market-entry experiment plan.

**Learning objectives:**

- Evaluate a new market using customer, channel, regulatory, and operational criteria.
- Identify the assumptions that make expansion risky.
- Design a small market-entry experiment before making a large commitment.

## Lesson 6.8 — Building a Competitive Moat

**Content:** A durable advantage becomes stronger as the business grows. Compare data, workflow, distribution, brand, network effects, switching costs, and operational excellence.

**COSA practice:** Map strategic advantages and risks in Strategy.

**Learner output:** Identify one current advantage and one potential future moat.

**Learning objectives:**

- Distinguish between a feature advantage and a durable competitive advantage.
- Identify potential moats in data, workflow, distribution, brand, and network effects.
- Invest in advantages that become stronger as the business grows.

## Lesson 6.9 — Operational Excellence and Governance

**Content:** Governance is not bureaucracy for its own sake. It makes important decisions, financial actions, legal obligations, AI-assisted work, and operational risk visible and accountable.

**COSA practice:** Use Approvals, Finance, Legal, Settings, Skills, Workflows, and Workspace Runtime to demonstrate controlled execution.

**Learner output:** Create a governance checklist for decisions, money, data, legal risk, and AI actions.

**Learning objectives:**

- Define the role of governance in a growing organization.
- Establish controls for approvals, financial decisions, legal obligations, and AI-assisted work.
- Balance execution speed with accountability, security, and risk management.
