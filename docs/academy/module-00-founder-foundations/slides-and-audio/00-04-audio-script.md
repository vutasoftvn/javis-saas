### [SLIDE 1 AUDIO] — Title & Core Thesis (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 1 (Slide 1: Cloud of thoughts condensing into a sharp project container.)
**Tone**: *Sharp, strategic, clarifying.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Ideas are cheap and infinite. [pause 0.5s] You can brainstorm a dozen brilliant startup concepts in an afternoon. But an idea cannot be executed, and an idea cannot be validated."
>
> "To make progress, you must convert an amorphous idea into a **Project**. [pause 0.5s] In COSA, a project is not a generic to-do list—it is a bounded venture bet with a specific customer, a specific hypothesis, and clear constraints."
>

### [SLIDE 2 AUDIO] — The Structural Hierarchy (30s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 2 (Slide 2: Four-tier hierarchy from Workspace down to Evidence.)
**Tone**: *Structured, educational.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Let us understand the structural hierarchy of your venture. [pause 0.5s] Your **Workspace** represents the entire company. Inside it, you create **Projects**, which represent distinct commercial experiments."
>
> "Within each project, you execute **Tasks**. [pause 0.5s] But remember this: tasks only produce outputs. What truly moves your venture forward is **Evidence**—the empirical proof that your hypothesis was correct."
>

### [SLIDE 3 AUDIO] — The Anatomy of a Project Brief (30s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 3 (Slide 3: Document preview highlighting the 5 essential fields.)
**Tone**: *Rigorous, disciplined.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Before writing any code or launching a campaign, you must draft a 5-point Project Brief. [pause 0.5s] Who is the exact customer? What is their painful friction? What is your proposed solution mechanism?"
>
> "And crucially, what is your **Stopping Rule**? [pause 0.5s] If you do not define what failure looks like in advance, you will spend months rationalizing poor results and burning precious runway."
>

### [SLIDE 4 AUDIO] — Boundary Enforcement (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 4 (Slide 4: Perimeter defense diagram showing In-Scope vs. Out-of-Scope.)
**Tone**: *Assertive, focused.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "The primary job of a project brief is to defend your boundaries. [pause 0.5s] Feature creep is the silent killer of early-stage startups."
>
> "Clearly designate what is in scope and what is explicitly out of scope. [pause 0.5s] Put non-essential ideas in the parking lot. You can always revisit them once your core assumption is validated."
>

### [SLIDE 5 AUDIO] — Anti-Patterns vs. Best Practices (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 5 (Slide 5: Contrast table showing infinite tinkering versus timeboxed milestones.)
**Tone**: *Cautionary, realistic.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Never let a project become an open-ended tinkering exercise. [pause 0.5s] When an MVP has no fixed deadline, founders keep polishing code to avoid the discomfort of customer rejection."
>
> "Set a strict timebox: two to four weeks. [pause 0.5s] At the end of that window, look at the evidence and make a disciplined decision: proceed, pivot, or stop."
>

### [SLIDE 6 AUDIO] — Founder Action Checkpoint (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 6 (Slide 6: Project Brief exercise card.)
**Tone**: *Action-oriented, closing.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "It is time to take action. [pause 0.5s] Open your COSA workspace, navigate to Strategy, and create your first official Project Brief."
>
> "Define your target customer, your core assumption, and three immediate validation tasks. [pause 0.5s] Lock in your venture bet, and let us start collecting real-world proof."
>
