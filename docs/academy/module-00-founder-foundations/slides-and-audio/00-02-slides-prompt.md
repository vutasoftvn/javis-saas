## SLIDE-BY-SLIDE SPECIFICATIONS

### Slide 1: Title & Definition (Hero Presentation)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-01 — Takeaway Claim`
- **Layout & Composition**: Hero layout with bold central definition on #070C18 dark canvas.
- **Header Badge**: `COSA ACADEMY · MODULE 00 · LESSON 0.2`
- **Main Headline**: **What Is a Startup? Searching Under Extreme Uncertainty**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: A startup is not a smaller version of a large company—it is a temporary organization searching for a repeatable business model.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Established companies execute known business models with predictable customers and unit economics.
  - Startups operate in extreme uncertainty: unknown customer problems, unproven value propositions, and untested pricing.
  - Your primary currency is not finished code—it is validated learning speed.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: THE STARTUP THESIS: Search, learn, and validate before you execute and scale.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Visual distinction: A rigid established grid versus a dynamic searching beam illuminating dark unknown terrain.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Minimalist graphic: On one side, rigid static blue corporate pillars; on the other, a bright focused teal beam piercing through dark abstract fog.*

### Slide 2: The Four Venture Archetypes (Taxonomy Matrix)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-02 — Definition Contrast`
- **Layout & Composition**: 4-quadrant structured grid on #0D172A containers.
- **Header Badge**: `CONCEPT TAXONOMY`
- **Main Headline**: **Idea vs. Project vs. Small Business vs. Startup**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Understanding the distinct dynamics of different economic initiatives.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - 1. An Idea: An untested opinion or intuition in the founder's mind. Zero commercial value on its own.
  - 2. A Project: A bounded initiative with specific scope, time, and deliverables.
  - 3. A Small Business: An established model executing in a known market (e.g., local consulting agency, bakery). High predictability, linear scaling.
  - 4. A Startup: An experiment designed for non-linear scale, searching for a repeatable and profitable customer engine.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: CRITICAL INSIGHT: Do not manage a search experiment with execution-stage corporate bureaucracy.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: 4-card matrix with color-coded risk and scalability indicators.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Four clean rectangular cards in 2x2 grid on dark navy background, subtle glow borders, minimalist icons representing thought bubble, clipboard, shopfront, and rocket.*

### Slide 3: The Uncertainty Curve (Visual Trajectory)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-03 — Operating Loop`
- **Layout & Composition**: Horizontal line chart comparing Capital Invested vs. Risk Reduction over time.
- **Header Badge**: `RISK REDUCTION`
- **Main Headline**: **The Startup Learning Curve**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Spend minimal capital while uncertainty is highest; scale capital only as risk decreases.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Early Stage (High Uncertainty): Problem & Solution risk dominate. Invest time in discovery interviews and manual prototypes.
  - Middle Stage (Moderate Uncertainty): Monetization & Channel risk dominate. Test willingness to pay and conversion economics.
  - Scale Stage (Low Uncertainty): Operational risk dominates. Invest capital into team expansion, marketing channels, and automation.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: RULE OF SURVIVAL: Cheap learning early preserves runway for when you discover true market traction.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Two diverging curves: A declining red risk line intersected by an ascending teal evidence line.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Financial chart on dark canvas: descending glowing red line (risk) crossing an ascending bright cyan curve (evidence), with labeled phase gates.*

### Slide 4: The Evidence Hierarchy (Data Hierarchy)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-04 — Focus Framework`
- **Layout & Composition**: Inverted pyramid showing levels of customer conviction.
- **Header Badge**: `SIGNAL INTEGRITY`
- **Main Headline**: **The Hierarchy of Customer Evidence**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Why stated customer compliments mean nothing compared to invested resources.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Level 1 (Lowest): Polite Opinions — 'That sounds like a great idea!' (High false positive rate).
  - Level 2: Stated Intent — 'I would definitely buy that if you built it.' (Still zero friction).
  - Level 3: Investment of Time & Reputation — Customer shares proprietary data, introduces their boss, or attends 3 workshops.
  - Level 4 (Highest): Financial Commitment — Upfront deposit, signed letter of intent, or active paid pilot.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: FILTER: Only trade product commitments for Level 3 and Level 4 customer evidence.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Tiered pyramid visual transitioning from muted slate at the bottom to vibrant cyan and teal at the peak.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Clean tiered pyramid diagram with 4 levels on dark slate background, glowing teal apex representing hard currency commitment.*

### Slide 5: Anti-Patterns vs. Best Practices (Comparative Matrix)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-06 — Decision Checkpoint`
- **Layout & Composition**: Side-by-side comparison table.
- **Header Badge**: `EVIDENCE CHECKS`
- **Main Headline**: **Vanity Progress vs. Real Startup Traction**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Distinguishing between genuine validation and self-deception.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Trap: Writing 10,000 lines of code before speaking to 10 buyers vs. Practice: Testing value propositions with no-code or wireframes.
  - Trap: Celebrating press mentions and awards vs. Practice: Tracking weekly active customer retention.
  - Trap: Scaling marketing before proving unit economics vs. Practice: Securing 5 referenceable ecstatic users first.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: DECISION RULE: If users do not complete their job successfully without your help, you are not ready to scale.
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Table comparing vanity milestones with empirical validation metrics.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Two column contrast table on dark background with rose crossmarks and teal checkmarks, high typography contrast.*

### Slide 6: Founder Action Checkpoint (Action Deliverable)
- **Visual Archetype**: `SL-07 — Learner Action`
- **Layout & Composition**: Card with structured template exercise.
- **Header Badge**: `EXERCISE: PROBLEM STATEMENT`
- **Main Headline**: **Formulate Your Falsifiable Customer Hypothesis**
- **Sub-headline / Thesis**: Translate your vague idea into a testable hypothesis statement.
- **Core Slide Content**:
  - Step 1: Identify your target customer segment with precision.
  - Step 2: Define the exact painful problem they experience today.
  - Step 3: Articulate the context or trigger event where the pain happens.
- **Highlight / Accent Box**: WRITE YOUR CORE HYPOTHESIS: 'We believe that [Target Customer] struggles with [Specific Problem] whenever [Context / Trigger].'
- **Diagram / Visual Structure**: Clean fill-in-the-blank card container with highlighted bracketed tokens.
- **AI Visual Generation Directive**: *Editorial template container with glowing teal input fields on deep navy card, modern UI card styling.*
