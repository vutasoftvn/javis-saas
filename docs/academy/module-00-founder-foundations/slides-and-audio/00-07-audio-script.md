## SLIDE-SYNCHRONIZED AUDIO SCRIPT

### [SLIDE 1 AUDIO] — Title & Core Thesis (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 1 (Slide 1: Glowing teal compass pointing to a singular target milestone.)
**Tone**: *Grounded, clear, pragmatic.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "OKRs—Objectives and Key Results—are famous in Silicon Valley, but most early-stage founders implement them completely wrong. [pause 0.5s] They copy the complex corporate systems used by Google and Intel, creating bureaucratic reporting spreadsheets that slow everything down."
>
> "In a startup, OKRs exist for one reason only: **radical focus**. [pause 0.5s] They answer the question: what is the single most important hurdle we must clear this cycle?"
>

### [SLIDE 2 AUDIO] — The Objective vs. Key Result Anatomy (30s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 2 (Slide 2: Split comparison between qualitative Objective and quantitative Key Results.)
**Tone**: *Instructional, precise.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Understand the distinction between the two parts. [pause 0.5s] Your **Objective** is qualitative, ambitious, and inspirational. It describes the destination—for example, 'Make our onboarding experience delightful'."
>
> "Your **Key Results** are quantitative, measurable, and un-fakeable. [pause 0.5s] They tell you whether you actually arrived. For example, 'Increase onboarding completion from 20% to 70%'. If you cannot measure it with hard numbers, it is not a Key Result."
>

### [SLIDE 3 AUDIO] — The OKR-to-Execution Cascade (30s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 3 (Slide 3: Vertical cascade connecting Vision, Project, OKR, and Tasks.)
**Tone**: *Architectural, systematic.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "How do OKRs connect to your everyday work? [pause 0.5s] Look at the cascade. Your high-level Strategy defines your multi-year vision. Your Project frames your current 12-week commercial bet."
>
> "Your OKRs set the quantifiable targets for that bet. [pause 0.5s] And your weekly sprint tasks are the specific activities you execute to move those key result meters. Every task you do on a Tuesday morning should trace directly back to an active Key Result."
>

### [SLIDE 4 AUDIO] — Common Early-Stage OKR Mistakes (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 4 (Slide 4: Three red warning containers detailing common OKR traps.)
**Tone**: *Cautionary, experienced.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Watch out for the three deadly OKR traps. [pause 0.5s] First, never write a task as a Key Result. Launching a new website is not a key result; acquiring 50 qualified leads from that website is."
>
> "Second, avoid goal overload. If you have five objectives and fifteen key results, you have no focus. [pause 0.5s] And third, review your key results weekly—never treat them as set-and-forget quarterly paperwork."
>

### [SLIDE 5 AUDIO] — Anti-Patterns vs. Best Practices (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 5 (Slide 5: Contrast table between corporate bureaucracy and lean startup agility.)
**Tone**: *Direct, coaching.*

> **Spoken Script**:
>
> "Remember that in an early-stage startup, missing an aspirational Key Result is not a moral failure. [pause 0.5s] It is diagnostic data."
>
> "If you executed all your planned tasks with high discipline but your retention metric didn't budge, that tells you something profound about your customer value proposition. [pause 0.5s] Learn from the numbers and adapt."
>

### [SLIDE 6 AUDIO] — Founder Action Checkpoint (25s)
**Slide Reference**: Slide 6 (Slide 6: Lean OKR setup card in COSA Strategy.)
**Tone**: *Action-oriented, inspiring.*

> **Spoken Script**:

>
> "Attach two or three quantifiable Key Results with baseline and target numbers. [pause 0.5s] Connect them to your primary Project Bet, align your team, and let us start moving the needle."
